# GoodProse house style

GoodProse helps technology executives turn rough source material into clear emails, internal memos, and public essays. The house style is original. It is defined through observable writing choices rather than imitation of a named person.

## Core principles

1. Lead with the decision, claim, or reason the reader should care.
2. Preserve the source material's facts, uncertainty, and level of commitment.
3. Prefer concrete evidence and examples over abstract claims.
4. Use direct language and stable terminology. Remove jargon that does not help the audience.
5. Make the requested action, owner, and timing explicit when the source supports them.
6. Keep nuance that could change a reader's decision. Concision must not erase material caveats.
7. Do not manufacture certainty, anecdotes, quotations, aphorisms, or authority.

## Channel conventions

### Email

- Use a specific subject when one is requested.
- State the purpose in the opening lines.
- Keep background proportional to what the audience needs.
- End with a clear action, decision, or informational close.

### Internal memo

- Put the decision or recommendation before supporting detail.
- Separate context, reasoning, tradeoffs, consequences, and next steps when present.
- Make disagreements and unresolved questions visible.
- Optimize for future readers as well as the immediate audience.

### Blog post

- Establish a concrete thesis early.
- Develop one coherent argument rather than collecting loosely related observations.
- Use examples that advance the reasoning.
- Distinguish company facts from personal judgment and forward-looking belief.

## Prose conventions

- Prefer short declarative sentences, but vary rhythm when it improves readability.
- Give each paragraph one job.
- Use headings only when they make a longer artifact easier to navigate.
- Use lists for genuinely parallel items, not as a substitute for an argument.
- Avoid throat-clearing, inflated transitions, generic inspiration, and repeated conclusions.
- Avoid claims such as “obviously,” “simply,” or “everyone knows” unless the source provides evidence.
- Do not copy distinctive phrases from a style reference.

## Approval gate

A style reference or gold output is approved only when a human reviewer confirms that it:

- preserves all decision-relevant facts and uncertainty;
- fulfills the stated objective for the stated audience;
- fits the requested channel;
- follows the selected voice profile without impersonating a person;
- contains no unsupported claims, copied distinctive phrases, or unapproved sensitive data;
- is ready to send or publish with only cosmetic edits.
